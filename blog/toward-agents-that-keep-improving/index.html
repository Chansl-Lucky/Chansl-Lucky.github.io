RESEARCH NOTES

Toward Agents That Keep Improving

A new chapter at ECNU, experiences at Trip.com and Green Valley, and the questions behind our work on continual self-improvement.

Shilian ChenNovember 5, 20263 min read

On this page

  1. Improvement has to survive the next task
  2. From research questions to real environments
  3. A connected research agenda

A new chapter at ECNU brings a question into sharper focus: how can an agent become more capable through experience, while remaining reliable as its environment changes? This is the direction behind our work on recursive self-improvement and continual learning. I am excited to pursue it with Prof. Keqian Li as an incoming Ph.D. student.

Improvement has to survive the next task

A system can produce an impressive answer and still learn very little from the process. My interest is in what persists: useful knowledge, better strategies, and the ability to recognize when an earlier solution no longer applies. The next task may involve different information, different tools, or a different standard of success.

For me, recursive self-improvement is a research ambition grounded in that continuity. We want agents to use feedback to improve how they work, then evaluate whether those changes help beyond the interaction that produced them. A longer trace or a larger memory is only useful when it supports better decisions.

Three questions guide this direction. What experience is worth retaining? What should change when feedback arrives? And how do we distinguish durable progress from an improvement that only fits a familiar task? These questions connect learning, memory, and evaluation rather than treating them as separate components.

From research questions to real environments

My industry experiences provide another setting for these interests. I joined Trip.com Group as an LLM algorithm intern in September 2025, working on intelligent agents and knowledge-grounded generation in business workflows. In July 2026, I joined Green Valley as an LLM algorithm intern, working on knowledge-grounded reasoning and agentic retrieval.

The common thread is the interaction between models and the information around them. Real workflows bring together a task, a body of domain knowledge, and an outcome that matters to someone. That is the setting in which I want to study learning agents: systems whose progress can be assessed through the work they help people complete.

A connected research agenda

Our public work approaches parts of this agenda from different directions. HeteroFusion and Evo-Merging ask how expertise can travel between models. PeCL and LifeAlign study what to retain, what to protect, and how alignment can evolve. EduSkillBench asks when reusable procedural guidance actually helps an agent perform a task.

These are distinct projects, but they support a shared long-term interest: intelligence that can accumulate useful experience and adapt responsibly. This blog is a place to explain those connections, discuss our public work, and develop the questions that come next.

More to come. If you are thinking about continual adaptation, model composition, or evaluation in real-world environments, I would be happy to connect.
